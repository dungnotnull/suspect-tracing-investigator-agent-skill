## Phased Questions

### Rapport
1. [Build rapport with the interviewee before substantive recall; explain the
   purpose, voluntary nature, and that "I don't know" is acceptable.]
2. [Establish baseline communication style; confirm they are comfortable taking
   time to think.]

### Open Narrative
3. [Ask the interviewee to recount everything they remember about {event}, from
   immediately before to immediately after, in their own words, without
   interruption, including details that seem trivial.]

### Context Reinstatement
4. [Mentally return the interviewee to the scene: surroundings, weather/lighting,
   sounds, smells, feelings, before further recall.]
5. [Ask the interviewee to describe their own physical location and viewpoint.]

### Varied Recall
6. [Recount the event in reverse chronological order.]
7. [Start the account from a different point in time and proceed forward.]

### Changed Perspective
8. [Describe the event from the perspective of someone else who was present.]

### Focused Follow-Up
9. [For each known fact: "Tell me more about..." — open-ended, no yes/no.]
10. ...

### Closing
- [Summarize the account back; ask for corrections.]
- [Ask whether anything else comes to mind; explain how to contact the
  investigator if later recall occurs.]
- [Document contemporaneously; avoid leading questions; record exact wording.]

## Reliability Considerations

- Avoid leading/suggestive questions; use open-ended phrasing.
- Do not interrupt the initial free narrative.
- Beware the misinformation effect (Loftus, 1996); do not introduce details the
  interviewee did not provide.
- Confidence ≠ accuracy; corroborate critical details independently.

## Post-Interview Actions

- Transcribe and store the recorded interview per chain-of-custody protocol.
- Cross-check key factual claims against independent evidence.
- Re-interview only if materially new information emerges.

## Legal & Ethical Boundaries

- Conduct only within lawful authority; voluntary participation.
- No coercion; document consent.
- The recorded interview is an evidence item — enter into chain of custody.
